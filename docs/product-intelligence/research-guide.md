# Research Guide

## Types of Research

### Discovery Calls
Initial conversations with prospects or contacts to understand their world. Objective: identify problems, workflows, and pain points.

### Customer Interviews
Structured conversations with existing customers. Objective: validate assumptions, gather feedback, identify improvement areas.

### Design Partner Reviews
Deep-dive sessions with design partners. Objective: validate features before build, gather implementation feedback.

### Conference Discussions
Informal conversations at industry events. Objective: market validation, trend identification, network expansion.

### Support Tickets
Inbound problem reports. Objective: identify product gaps, documentation needs, training opportunities.

## Capturing Knowledge

### During the Conversation
1. Listen for problems — "This takes too long", "We use Excel for that"
2. Note workarounds — "So we export to CSV and match manually"
3. Capture business impact — "This adds 3 days to our close"
4. Ask about frequency — "How often does this happen?"
5. Probe for severity — "What happens when it goes wrong?"

### After the Conversation
1. Create a Conversation record with summary and key insights
2. Extract each problem into a Contribution
3. Link contributions to relevant modules
4. Create Evidence records for validated problems
5. Create FeatureRequest records for requested features

## Question Framework

### Problem Discovery
- What does your current workflow look like?
- What tools do you use?
- What takes the most time?
- What frustrates you most?
- What would you change if you could?

### Solution Validation
- Does this problem sound familiar?
- How would you solve it?
- What would the ideal solution look like?
- What would you NOT want in a solution?
- What would make you trust it?

### Priority Assessment
- How much time does this waste per week/month?
- What's the business impact?
- How many people are affected?
- Is this getting worse?
- What have you tried already?
