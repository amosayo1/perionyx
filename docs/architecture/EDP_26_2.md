## Key Decisions

### Decision 1: CERTIFIED WITH CONDITIONS (not CERTIFIED)

**Rationale**: The architecture is sound but 5 AT-RISK domains exceed the 2-domain threshold for full certification. The singleton overwrite and cross-tenant leak are certificate-blocking.

**Alternatives Considered**:
- CERTIFIED: Rejected — 5 AT-RISK domains indicate systemic gaps
- NOT CERTIFIED: Rejected — architecture is genuinely good (7.2/10)
- FOUNDATION REWORK REQUIRED: Rejected — the issues are implementation gaps, not architectural flaws

### Decision 2: 3-Phase Remediation (not all-at-once)

**Rationale**: Phased remediation allows independent verification of each condition. 26.2A (singleton + tenant isolation) addresses the 2 CRITICAL conditions first.

**Phases**:
- 26.2A: C-01 + C-02 (1 week)
- 26.2B: C-03 + C-04 + C-07 (1-2 weeks)
- 26.2C: C-05 + C-06 (1 week)

### Decision 3: Escalation Path (not indefinite)

**Rationale**: Without escalation, conditions can linger indefinitely. Week 6 escalation to CTO creates accountability.

### Decision 4: 25-Domain Certification (not fewer)

**Rationale**: Enterprise foundation certification requires comprehensive evaluation. 25 domains across 5 clusters ensure no blind spots.

| Pros | Cons |

| | Would delay Phase 26.2D by months |

### Alternative C: Certified With Conditions (Selected)

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Acknowledges sound architecture | Requires 3-4 weeks of remediation |
| Creates accountability (escalation path) | 6 conditions must be tracked |
| Allows phased remediation | Not production-ready until conditions met |

**Verdict**: Right balance of recognition and accountability.
