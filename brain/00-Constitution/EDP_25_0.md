# EDP-25.0: Brain Knowledge Platform Restructure

## Decision

Restructure the Brain from 16 loosely-organized folders (v1) to 20 formally-governed folders (v2) with constitutional authority, page standards, knowledge graph requirements, and customer intelligence system.

## Context

The Brain grew organically across 24 phases of development. By Phase 25.0, it contained 129 files across 16+ folders with:

- **No governing constitution** — no rules for content creation, validation, or archival
- **No page standards** — inconsistent frontmatter, section counts, link patterns
- **7 empty stub files** at root level (`brain/01-*.md` through `brain/07-*.md`)
- **4 Obsidian canvas/base files** with no content (`.canvas`, `.base`)
- **Duplicate numbering** between old and new folder schemes (two `04-`, two `08-`, two `13-`, two `14-`)
- **Customer discovery limited** to 1 interview (Adeel Aslam) and a skeleton MOC
- **Engineering lessons (44)** confined to a single subfolder with no structure
- **ADRs (22+)** in a separate structure from lessons and decisions
- **No knowledge graph requirements** — pages were isolated, not connected

The Brain was accumulating knowledge but not connecting it. Without governance, the accumulation would eventually become noise.

## Alternatives Considered

### Option A: Incremental Cleanup
- Just delete empty stubs and standardize naming
- **Pros**: Minimal disruption, fast to implement
- **Cons**: Doesn't solve structural problems, no governance, no customer intelligence, no knowledge graph

### Option B: Full Restructure (Selected)
- 20-folder architecture with constitutional authority
- **Pros**: Scalable, governable, customer intelligence ready, knowledge graph enabled
- **Cons**: Migration effort, temporary duplication during transition

### Option C: External Tool (Notion, Confluence)
- Move knowledge to external platform
- **Pros**: Better search, collaboration features, permissions
- **Cons**: Loss of code proximity, markdown portability, version control, git-based workflow

## Decision

**Option B: Full restructure** — 20-folder architecture with constitutional governance.

## Rationale

1. **Governance before scale**: The Brain needs governance before it can scale. Without rules, every new phase adds more unstructured content.
2. **Customer intelligence requires structure**: Customer discovery is not just an interview folder — it requires People, Companies, Interviews, Pain Points, Evidence, Hypotheses, and Competitive Signals.
3. **Knowledge graph ensures connectivity**: Pages must link to each other. Isolated pages are knowledge silos. Graph requirements enforce cross-referencing.
4. **Constitutional authority means enforcement**: Rules are not suggestions. The constitution defines what the Brain must contain and how it must be maintained.
5. **Templates ensure consistency**: Every new page starts from a template, reducing variation and ensuring all content types are covered.

## Consequences

### Migration Actions
| Action | Count | Details |
|--------|-------|---------|
| Files reorganized | 193 | Distributed across 20 folders |
| Lessons migrated | 44 | From single subfolder to `17-Lessons/` |
| ADRs migrated | 22+ | From separate structure to `11-Decisions/` |
| Empty stubs archived | 9 | Moved to `19-Archive/` |
| Canvas/base files archived | 4 | Moved to `19-Archive/` |
| Old folder indexes archived | 10 | Superseded by new MOCs |
| New governance docs created | 10 | Constitution, standards, guides, templates |

### New Systems Created
- **Knowledge Constitution** — 10 core laws governing all Brain content
- **Page Standards** — frontmatter, section, linking, and tag requirements
- **Knowledge Graph Guide** — cross-cluster linking, path length targets, MOC structure
- **Customer Intelligence System** — 7 categories with templates and linking rules
- **10 page templates** — one per major content type
- **Folder MOCs** — 20 entry points with link inventories and review schedules

### Positive Consequences
- Every page is governed by constitutional authority
- Every page links to at least 2 other pages in different clusters
- Customer intelligence scales with the team
- New team members can navigate the Brain via MOCs
- Quarterly reviews keep content fresh

### Negative Consequences
- Migration requires careful link rewriting
- Some wikilinks may temporarily break during transition
- Template-driven content may feel rigid initially
- Quarterly review process adds maintenance overhead
