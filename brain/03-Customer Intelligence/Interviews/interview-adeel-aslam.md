# Interview — Adeel Aslam

**Date**: 2026-07-21
**Channel**: Direct message
**Context**: Customer discovery outreach response

## Summary

Adeel Aslam is a finance professional in real estate and construction. Responding to a discovery question from Amos, he identified **vendor invoice reconciliations and approval workflows** as the primary manual pain point in his finance operations.

## Key Quotes

> "In real estate and construction finance, one area we still manage manually is vendor invoice reconciliations and approval workflows."

> "While our systems cover reporting and cash flow planning fairly well, these operational tasks often require manual oversight to ensure accuracy."

## Pain Points Identified

| Pain Point | Severity | Current State | Perionyx Coverage |
|-----------|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Vendor invoice reconciliation | High | Manual | Partial — 3-way matching exists but end-to-end workflow broken |
| Approval workflows | High | Manual oversight | Partial — approval matrix exists but not wired to payment execution |

## Insights

1. **Reporting and cash flow planning are "fairly well" covered** — his existing systems handle these. This validates that reporting is table stakes, not a differentiator.
2. **Operational tasks are the gap** — the manual work is in the transactional layer (invoice matching, approvals), not the analytical layer. This aligns with Phase 20.0 finding: modules exist but workflows don't connect.
3. **Real estate/construction is a vertical** — industry-specific needs (progress billing, retainage, lien waivers, project-based accounting) are not addressed by generic ERP. This is a potential vertical opportunity.
4. **"Manual oversight to ensure accuracy"** — he doesn't trust automated matching. This maps to the Trust principle: accuracy must be provable, not assumed.
