# Lesson 54 — Customer Knowledge Compounds

## Statement

Customer knowledge compounds when every conversation becomes structured evidence.

## Context

Before Phase 27.0, the Brain had 11 placeholder People profiles (all TBD), 10 placeholder Interviews (all TBD), zero Companies, zero Pain Points, zero Validated Evidence, and zero cross-system contacts between CRM and Brain. The CRM had 19 operational contacts with rich data that was invisible to the knowledge layer.

Phase 27.0 imported all 39 contacts into structured profiles with 15+ sections each, created 39 interview records, mapped knowledge graph connections, and established evidence traceability from interviews → evidence → principles → product decisions.

## Evidence

- 39 contacts imported: 17 LinkedIn, 19 CRM, 3 existing
- 8 market themes identified (T1-T8), 2 promoted to Validated (T3, T5)
- 9 product principles (P1-P9), 1 promoted to Validated (P3)
- 7 design partner candidates pre-scored
- Knowledge graph: People × Pain Points × Workflows × Evidence × Principles
- Every product principle now traces to at least one evidence source

## Principle

The value of customer intelligence is not in the volume of contacts but in the structure of the knowledge. A structured profile with 15 fields is worth more than 100 LinkedIn connections with no structure. When every conversation becomes structured evidence, the evidence compounds — patterns emerge, contradictions surface, and product decisions become traceable.

## Implications

1. **Structure before content** — Building the People/Interviews/Evidence structure before importing transcripts made every import systematic and traceable
2. **CRM is operational, Brain is knowledge** — The CRM tracks who we've contacted; the Brain tracks what we've learned. Both are necessary, neither is sufficient
3. **Evidence compounds** — One interview gives a hypothesis. Two interviews give a pattern. Three interviews give a validated principle. The compounding requires structure to detect
4. **Knowledge graphs reveal gaps** — When you map People → Evidence → Principles, the gaps become visible. You can see which principles lack evidence, which themes lack sources, which contacts lack interviews
